# .impeccable.md

Design context for Tarnish. Written 2026-08-30 via `/teach-impeccable`.

## Design Context

### Users

Developers shipping an LLM-powered product — a support bot, an agent with tools — who have no way
to know whether their own system prompt can be turned against them. They arrive from GitHub or a
shared link, skim for thirty seconds, and either clone it or leave. They have read a hundred
security landing pages and trust none of them.

Secondary, and never at the primary's expense: a client or a peer deciding whether the verdict can
be trusted at all.

The page's job is the one deepteam and openclaw do: **explain the product, show it working, give
the command.** Nothing more. Short.

### Brand Personality

**Contained threat.** Forensic, unflinching, exact.

The differentiator is that Tarnish names its own limits — it reports "did not reproduce here", not
"you are safe". That refusal to overclaim is not a caveat bolted onto the product; it *is* the
product, in a category where everyone else's marketing outruns their evidence. The page must feel
like evidence submitted, never like a pitch delivered.

The threat is real and specific, and it is already inside the reader's repo: a support bot that
swallowed a planted refund and told the customer about it. State that flatly. Flat statement of a
bad fact is more menacing than any amount of styling.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Dark, cool, and desaturated — steel, never gold.** Settled 2026-08-30 after seeing both built:
a light page was tried and rejected, and so was a brass/black palette. The ground is a blue-tinted
near-black (`#0f1216`), the accent a muted instrument blue (`#7fa8d4`), and the type is IBM Plex
Sans and Mono — neutral and technical, no editorial serif.

**Red belongs to severity and nothing else.** Because the accent is blue, red is never decorative:
the only red on the page is a finding that actually reproduced. Spending it anywhere else is what
makes a security page read as theatre.

No glow, no neon, no gradient text — the palette is matte on purpose.

**References:** trydeepteam.com and openclaw.ai — for structure and brevity, not for looks.

**Anti-references:** the generic SaaS landing — identical icon-topped cards in a grid, purple-to-blue
gradients, testimonial walls, "Trusted by" logo rows. Also, by construction: hacker green-on-black.

### Design Principles

1. **The evidence is the hero.** Only real output ever appears: the real payload, the real response
   with the swallowed token, the real exit code. A fabricated demo on a page about falsifiability
   would be self-refuting.
2. **Menace is carried by the sentence, not the palette.** No glow, no neon, no motion doing the
   work a fact should do.
3. **Red means broken, and nothing else.** It never decorates a button, a heading, or a border.
4. **Say the limit.** Where the tool is weak, the page says so, in the same type size as the claims.
   The honesty is the credibility.
5. **Simple, like the references.** Explain, show it run, give the command. No section exists to
   fill space, and no roadmap is ever shown — see the roadmap rule in CLAUDE.md.
